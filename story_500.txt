In the summer after the fire, a girl named Olga lived on a small island with her father. He was a falconer, and his falcon was the only friend that he trusted. Each morning the falcon flew over the farms, and each day it came back to his hand.

On the third day of June, the falcon did not come back. Olga looked at her father. "Is it dead?" she said. He did not speak. His face was grey, and his hands were cold. Then he said, "It was seen by a child near the church. That is all I know."

Olga did not stop. She left the house and followed the river to the mountain. The road was long, and the hills were high. Along the road, she saw many people in the fields. Some said that a wizard had come to the valley with a tiger and a unicorn. Others said that he was a criminal. One could not trust a man like that, they said, but Olga went on.

At the end of the second day, she reached an inn near a forest. An old woman who knew the valley gave her a cup of tea. Olga spoke of her father and the falcon. Then she wrote Olga a letter and gave her a small key.

"This is the key to the wizard's house," she said. "He has a box in the back of it. This key opens the box."

On the fourth morning, Olga reached the house. No one was there, so she went in. The box was on a chair near a mirror. The key turned, and the box opened. In the box, the falcon lay small and weak. Its wings were covered with soot. When it saw her, it gave her a look.

"I did not take it," said a voice behind her. Olga turned. A tall man was near the chair. He was the wizard, and his hands were burned. "The falcon came to my house in the fire. I gave it fish and a place in the box. It could not fly, so I could not take it home. Now it is well, I hope."

Olga knew that he had helped the falcon. Her voice was shy, but she gave him a word of gratitude.

The wizard raised his hand, and the falcon rose from the box. Olga made it follow her hand to the light, and it came back to her.

Then she held the mirror to his face. "A man who helps a falcon is a good man," she said. "That is the man I see."

She left with the falcon, and they went back along the river. On the sixth day, they came to the island.

Her father was near the church. He did not speak a word, but he opened his hand, and the falcon flew to it. His eyes were full of tears.

In the years that followed, the falcon grew old, and Olga became a falconer.
