The Dwarf of the Mountain

In the third summer after the old king died, a girl named Louise lived in a village near a high mountain. Her father was a woodsman, and her mother was dead. The river gave the village its fish and its food, but that summer it grew small. Each morning the farmers came to the river, and each morning the river was lower. The fish were dead, and the fields were red.

Louise looked at her father. "Will the river die?" she said.

"I do not know," he said. "Some say that a witch lives on the mountain. Others say that a dwarf has taken the river. One cannot trust a word of it."

That day the farmers came to the inn, and a vote was held. Many words were spoken, and the inn was full. At last Maria, a widow, spoke to the people. She was the oldest woman in the village, and they trusted her. "The river came from the mountain," she said, "and the mountain can take it back. One will go to the top and speak with the dwarf."

No one spoke. The farmers looked at their hands. Then Louise said, "I will go."

Her father did not want this. "A child cannot go," he said. "The road is long, and the top of the mountain is cold."

"I know that," said Louise. "But the river is the life of the village, and I am shy, not weak."

Maria gave Louise a cup of tea and was near her. The dwarf was old, she said, and he was a good man, but he was not well. The people of the village had forgotten the king, and she said that he wanted the old song, the song that the king liked. Maria knew it, and she said that she would teach it to Louise.

For three days Maria made Louise learn the song. It was an old song, and its words were from an old language. On the fourth day, her father gave her rice and honey for the road. His hands were old, and his voice was low. "Come home," he said.

Louise left at the first light of the morning. The summer burned the hills, and the river was only a small line in the fields. Near the village, a butterfly with golden wings came out of the fields. It flew ahead of her, then it came back, then it flew ahead again. "Is it a guide?" said Louise. The butterfly did not speak, but it flew along the river, and she followed it.

On the second day, the road went into a forest. The forest was deep, and the air was cool. Louise went on for a long time, and she was weak, but she did not turn back.

On the third day, the road went up into the hills, and the hills became rock. The wind was cold and high. A tiger lay on the road. The tiger looked at her, and its eyes were large and golden. She was shy, but she opened her mouth and gave the tiger the first words of the song. The tiger turned its eyes away, and then it went off into the rocks. The butterfly came back to her hand, and she went on.

On the fourth day, she reached the top. An old mine was in the side of the mountain, and from it came the voice of the river, deep and high. Louise went in. In the middle of the mine, a dwarf was on a chair of iron. A great rock was before him, and behind the rock the river moved.

The dwarf looked at her. He was small, and his face was like old marble. "Who is this girl?" he said. "Does she come for the river?"

"I am Louise, from the village near the river," she said. "The farmers are weak, and the fish are dead. Is the river held in this mine?"

"It is," said the dwarf. "I was the king's friend. For many years, the people of the valley played music by the river, and the king came to the river. Then the king died, and the music stopped. Now the people have forgotten him. This rock was made by these hands, and it will be there until the song is given."

Louise did not speak at once. Her hands were cold, and her voice was small. Then she looked at the dwarf's face, and she was not shy. She opened her mouth and gave the old song her voice.

The words went into the mine like a light. The dwarf did not move. His eyes were full, and he took his hand to his face. When the song was finished, he said, "That is the song. That is the one that the king liked." For a long time he did not speak. Then he rose and raised his hand.

The rock fell. The river ran out of the mine and along the mountain, high and cold and deep. Louise looked at the river, and the dwarf looked at it with her, and the butterfly flew over it.

"Go home," said the dwarf. "Teach the song to the children. Make them play it by the river each summer, and I will take it no more."

Louise went along the river to the village, and on the fourth day of the road she came home. The people were at the river, and it was high. Her father was with them. He did not say a word, but he opened his hands, and she ran to him.

That summer, the fields grew, and the fish came back. Maria taught the song to the children of the village, and Louise played it by the river. In the years that followed, it became the song of the valley. Each year, on the first day of summer, the people came to the river and played the old music for the king and for the dwarf.

And the river did not stop.
