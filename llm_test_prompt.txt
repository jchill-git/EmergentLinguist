=== Text in English ===

1. Jon was a young shepherd, and he lived with his sheep on the side of a high, rocky mountain. He knew every field and every tree on that mountain, and the dark did not trouble him. But on one long, dark hour, something was different. The moon was full, the snow lay over the field, and the sky was full of stars. The sheep would not rest. They kept close together, and their eyes were wide.

2. Jon took his lamp and went out. At first he saw no one. Then he heard a sound, long and low, and he knew it was a wolf. A wolf could kill many sheep in one nighttime, and Jon had no help. He held his lamp high and spoke to the animals, and they came to him.

3. The wolf came closer. Jon could see its eyes in the light of the lamp. He did not move, and he did not call out. He kept the sheep behind him, a single young man with a single lamp, and he held the eyes of the wolf for a long time.

4. The wolf was also very old. Jon saw this, and he knew. It had come to the mountain to hunt for food, not to fight. He gave it the last of his food, and the wolf took it and went back into the dark.

5. When the sun rose, Jon was happy. The sheep were all there, and the wolf did not return.

6. Samuel was a sailor, and for three years he had been far from home. Every year he had written a letter to his wife, Mary, but he never knew if she had them. Now the ferry reached the harbour, and the sun was low over the sea.

7. He went along the road to the village. The church was there, and the old clock on it was broken. A child was playing in the square, and Samuel knew him. It was his son, Paul, who had been very young when he went to sea. The child did not know him. “Who is this man?” Paul called out. Samuel replied that he was a sailor, who had come a long way to see his family.

8. Paul saw him for a long time. Then he took the sailor by the hand and led him along the road, past the trees and the fields, to a home with a lamp in the window. The lamp was in the window, though the sun was still in the sky.

9. Mary was at the window with her hand on the table. She spoke no word. She had kept that lamp in the window every hour of every year, and she had known that he would come back. Samuel held out his hand, and at last she took it.

10. They were together for a long time. Behind them, the old clock in the square began to move, for the first time in three years.
