In the first year of the long winter, a young woman named Anna lived in a small village by the river. Her father was a farmer, and her brother served the king in the town. Anna wanted to see the world, but the road was long and the hills were cold.

One morning, a letter was found near the church. It was written by her brother. Some said that the king was dead, and others said that he was very weak. Anna looked at her mother. Can she find the road in winter? her mother asked. Her father did not speak a word. Then he made her learn the names of the rivers and the hills. This took three days. On the fourth day he found her a friend, a tall man who knew the mountain road.

They left at the second light of the morning. The man held a key, and he gave it to her. That key opened the king's house, he said. On the way, a snake lay in the road, and the man burned the old wood near it. The snake went away.

At the end of the sixth day, they came to the town. One of the people looked at Anna. This is the woman, she said. Anna was very shy, but she had her friend, her key, and her brother's letter.

The king was not dead. He had a cold, and he was good to her. He gave her a place in the house.
